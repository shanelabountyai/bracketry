# Bracketry write-up

Finished at L-022 (2026-10-12). The sections below are the summary; the per-item notes after them are the record they were drawn from. Closure artifacts are listed in `docs/RELEASE_NOTES.md`.

## What it is

A rec-league scheduler built from 2026-10-06 to 2026-10-12 on `saas-foundation` v1.2.0. The software writes a season under constraints, ranks teams through a tiebreak chain, seeds and plays a single-elimination bracket, and reschedules a rained-out day. The engine in `src/lib/league/` is pure and deterministic under a seed; the app loads rows, calls it, and stores what it returns.

## What it is not

- **Not optimal, and it never says so (D-5).** A greedy draft and a seeded local search, no solver library (D-2). On the nine-team capstone the search finishes at 114 at seed 2027 against a floor of 100 shown by counting; seeds 1, 2 and 3 finish at 110, 102 and 120. None reaches the floor, and the floor counts the rest rule only.
- **Synthetic data, not deployed (D-36).** Eastside Rec Softball is invented and the three-way tie is staged. Nothing bills except CI minutes (D-37).
- **Unstyled.** The screens are plain HTML with no design pass.
- **Not built:** umpires, rosters, registration, payments, live scoring, double elimination, coach accounts, a scorekeeper role (D-30), an editor for points and forfeit settings. Notices are queued, not sent.
- **Desk research.** No commissioner has reviewed the rules or the defaults.

## Measured at closure

Gate on 2026-10-12: lint 0 errors (1 warning, the template's), typecheck clean, 383 tests across 52 files, `foundation:drift` clean against v1.2.0. 35 decisions were logged during the build (D-1 to D-35); D-36 and D-37 are closure. 18 of 18 constraint tests write a conflicting row with raw SQL and name the constraint that refused it. The demo season: 51 games in 56 slots, soft cost 301 before repair and 122 after, 4,060 iterations, 29 accepted moves, floor 100.

## Defects found

Each was found by running something larger than a unit test, and each is in the per-item notes with its numbers.

| Found at | Defect | How it was found | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-010 | A rainout on Thu Apr 15 moved a game to Tue Apr 13, two days before the rain. | Reading the output of the first end-to-end capstone run. | The engine has no clock by design, so the caller keeps past open slots out of the inventory (D-23); the app never offers a slot on or before today. |
| L-017 | A draft made before a slot was closed or a score entered could still be published. | Writing the rainout action and asking what a stale draft does. | Publish refuses a draft with an unplayed game in a closed slot or a moved game that has since been scored (D-31). |
| L-021 | The demo league gave 133 on one run and 122 on the next at the same seed. | Running the seed three times. | Team, division and field ids were random and the engine sorts by id; the seed now uses fixed ids (D-35). |
| L-018 | The template's invariant sweep timed out at 180 s after four new actions. | The gate. | Two missing fixture rows; with them the sweep takes 11 s. Why it showed as a timeout and not as the harness's own "no fixture row" finding was not established. |
| L-019 | A new integration test was red and the test was wrong. | The gate. | With one game scored, second place was a three-way tie, so `generateBracket` was right to refuse the top 2. The test was fixed, not the code. |
| L-027 | An untouched score form saved a 0-0 tie. | A scorekeeper persona review of the screens, after closure. | The form posted 0 in both boxes by default and the action also read an empty box as 0. The boxes now start empty and the action refuses an empty box unless a forfeit is chosen (D-44). |
| L-009 | `pending:check` crashed once no pending suites were left. | Running it after the last suite went green. | It now says so and exits 0. |

## Hardest bug

The rainout into the past (L-010). Every reschedule test passed: the right games moved, no other game moved, the chosen slot broke no rule and cost least. None asked whether the chosen day had already happened, because the engine is forbidden a clock (D-3) and the spec for `reschedule` did not say who keeps the past out. It appeared only when one command ran the whole season as a story and the output read "moved to Tue Apr 13" under a rainout dated Thu Apr 15. Nothing in `src/lib/league` changed. The fix was to name the owner of time: the caller filters the inventory, and the app applies the same rule in the season's timezone. The lesson kept: when a component is made simple by leaving something out, the thing left out needs a named owner, and an end-to-end run is what finds the gap.

The unexplained one is smaller and still open: the first full `npm test` after the L-011 migration took 369 s and the second 29 s, with no memory pressure, no lock cancellations in the Postgres log, and a TRUNCATE that timed at 25 ms. The cause is unknown and it has not recurred.

## What is open

P1: L-023 (what-if standings, clinched and eliminated on points by enumeration up to 12 open games) and L-024 (a calendar feed per team) were built after closure; L-026 (crossover games between divisions, counted in each team's own standings) followed; L-025 (the LLM benchmark) ran once; its table is in the per-item notes. A review from the three personas' chairs on 2026-10-12 added fifteen unbuilt rows (L-027 to L-041) to the backlog; the largest is that a team cannot be edited or withdrawn after it is added. Known gaps, all in `NEXT.md`: plain `generate` ignores played games; `publish` does not look at playoff games; public reads share the template's per-IP limit; the seed was only tried on a Monday; a playoff game cannot be scored live in the demo.

## Per-item notes

- **L-022:** closure added no product code. The exec brief leads with what the project is not, takes its numbers from a fresh gate run rather than from these notes, and shows the unstyled screens as they are.

- **L-002:** guard rails make the engine's purity (D-3) a lint error rather than a convention, and `pending:check` makes "red for the right reason" (D-20) a number: 133 of 138 pending tests fail with NotImplementedError, 5 fail on their assertion.
- **L-004:** feasibility reports necessary conditions only and says so: a pass reads "these inputs could fit", and each shortfall is a sentence with its numbers (9 teams over 4 weeks: 36 games, 32 slots, 4 short).
- **L-003:** the round robin is checked for every size from 2 to 16 rather than assumed complete: every pair once (twice in a double), nobody twice in a round, byes spread one per team, home/away within 1 (exactly 0 in a double).
- **L-005:** one function judges every schedule (D-4), and its counting was agreed in plain words before it was coded. The deliberately bad fixture scores exactly what the spec's hand arithmetic says: 6 hard, 31 soft, one game unplaced. The one gap the spec left, a pinned game that is not placed at all, was settled as "unplaced, not a pin violation" (D-21).
- **L-006:** the generator is a greedy draft plus a seeded local search, and it reports its work rather than claiming a best answer (D-5). On the 9-team capstone the draft scores 320 and the search finishes at 114 at seed 2027 (2,508 iterations, 22 accepted moves, 281 ms), against a floor of 100 that can be shown by counting. Seeds 1, 2 and 3 finish at 110, 102 and 120; none reaches the floor. The move set and how order is kept deterministic were agreed before the loop was written, and a test shows that reordering the inputs does not change the schedule.
- **L-007:** a rainout moves only the games that lost their slot (D-12), and a test checks every other game one by one. Each displaced game goes to the free slot that breaks no rule and costs least, earliest on a tie, or is listed as unplaced when there is none. It is one pass, a game at a time, with no random choice, so the seed in the request goes unused. Raining out Thu Apr 22 on the capstone displaces 1 game; with four Saturday makeup slots it moves there and the season scores 107 (114 before the rainout), and with no makeup slots it moves to a spare Thursday slot and the score stays 114. 3 ms.
- **L-008:** standings are computed from results on every read (D-13), and the tiebreak chain is a short ordered list walked by one recursive function (D-14). Head-to-head among the tied teams is the same tally run on fewer teams, so there is one counting rule, not two. A tied-scope step is skipped until every tied pair has met equally: in fixture ST-8, applying head-to-head mid-season would have put A first, and the skip puts C first on +7. A tie nothing breaks is a shared rank with a flag until a commissioner's decision is logged; the code has no random draw to fall back on. All nine hand-worked fixtures passed with no expected value changed.
- **L-009:** the playoff bracket is a plain tree built from one recurrence: each seed `s` is followed by `size + 1 − s`, which keeps seeds 1 and 2 in opposite halves and hands every bye to a top seed without a special case. Recording a result returns a new bracket and never changes the old one, and a correction is allowed only until the next game in that line has been played (BRKT-05). Ties are refused and a forfeit advances the other side whatever the runs say. All 17 hand-worked tests passed with no expected value changed.
- **L-010:** one command tells the whole story with no database: `npm run league -- capstone --seed 2027` checks the season fits, generates it (320 down to 114 against a floor of 100), plays it with a forced three-way tie that the tiebreak chain resolves in two steps, seeds six teams, plays the bracket to a champion and rains out the busiest day. A test runs it twice and compares every character. Writing it found a gap nothing else had: the rainout moved one game to a date two days before the rain, because the engine has no clock and nobody had said who keeps the past out of the makeup list. The caller does now (D-23). With four Saturday makeup slots the season scores 81 after the rain, under the floor, because the makeup day is an eighth week.
- **L-011:** the rules that must never break are in the database, not in the app: one game per slot and one slot per game within a version, one published version per season, and results that can only be added to. A correction is a new row, and three constraints together keep each game's corrections in one line with exactly one current result. 18 tests write the conflicting rows directly and name the constraint that refused each one. The plan (tables and exact SQL) was agreed before any of it was written.
- **L-012:** setup is a form over rules the database already enforces: every duplicate (a team name, a slot, a blackout) comes back as a sentence mapped from the unique violation, never a check first. A weekly pattern is materialized by one pure function over day strings, and the authorization harness drove all eleven actions with no per-action code, which only worked after format checks moved behind the scoped lookup so a foreign id is always "not found".
- **L-013:** the generator now has a screen, and the screen says only what was measured: how many hard breaks, unplaced games and soft points the draft had, how many it had after repair, how many tries and accepted moves that took, and every remaining problem in a sentence. A blank seed picks a new one and the seed is stored, so any draft can be reproduced exactly; the same league and seed gave the same fingerprint twice. A season that cannot fit, or that is over the limits (16 teams, 200 games, 10,000 tries), is refused with the numbers before anything runs. A 12-team, 66-game season took under half a second.
- **L-015:** a commissioner can enter a game's score, or mark a forfeit, once the game's day has come in the league's own timezone. A second score is refused unless it is marked as a correction with a reason; the earlier score is never overwritten and the full history stays visible. Letting a plain team scorekeeper do this needs a change to the shared starter, so for now it is the commissioners.
- **L-016:** standings are worked out fresh every time the page opens, from each game's latest score, so a corrected score changes the table at once and nothing can drift. Every team's row says why it sits where it does ("2nd via head-to-head over Cubs"). When teams are level and no tiebreaker separates them, the table shows a shared rank flagged for the commissioner, who records the order and how it was decided (coin flip or drawn lots). The software never picks. The tiebreaker order can be changed; once games are scored a change needs a reason and is logged.
- **L-017:** when rain takes a day, the commissioner closes it, adds makeup slots if there are any, and asks for a reschedule. Only the games that lost their slot move, and the action checks that itself on every run rather than trusting the engine's tests. A game that already has a score is never moved, and a free slot on or before today is never offered. A game with nowhere legal to go is listed as not placed with the reason counted out ("None of the 2 free slots is legal: 1 on a day one of the teams is blacked out, 1 on a day one of the teams already plays"), and that draft cannot be published. Publishing sends each affected team one notice naming the game, the old slot and the new one. "Regenerate the rest of the season" is a separate button with a warning and a tick box, because it may move games no rain touched. Writing it found a hole in publishing: a draft made before a slot was closed or a score entered could still be published, so publish now refuses both.
- **L-018:** playoffs. The commissioner picks how many teams qualify and the bracket is seeded from the standings at that moment, then frozen with a fingerprint of the table it came from. If a season score is corrected later, the page says so and says whether the seeds would change; it never reseeds on its own, and regenerating is allowed only until the first playoff score. A tie that needs a commissioner decision blocks seeding for the teams inside the cut. Playoff games are placed by the same engine call as a rainout, treated as games that lost their slot, so the one scoring function decides what is legal and no other game moves; a later round is placed once its teams are known. A score feeds the winner forward, a tie is refused, a forfeit advances the other team, and a result can be corrected until the next game in its line is scored. After that the later result has to be cleared first, which needed one new engine function. The database rows are never trusted as the bracket: every change replays the stored scores over the frozen seeds, under a row lock.
- **L-014:** a schedule can now be published, and only a clean one can: no broken rules and no unplaced games, plus a reason that is kept with it. Publishing a new version retires the old one but keeps it viewable, and the database itself refuses a second published version in a season. Two versions can be compared, game by game, showing which games changed slot. A commissioner can pin a game to a slot; a pin that breaks a rule on its own (a blackout, a double booking) is refused with the sentence saying why, and the next generation works around the pins that are set.
- **L-019:** the public page. Coaches and players have no account, so each season with a published schedule has one page anyone can read: schedule by day or by team, standings with the reason for each rank, and the bracket. Drafts never reach it. A game that moved in the latest version says where it moved from for 7 days. The data goes through projections that start empty and add public fields by name, and a test puts a contact email on every team and fails if it, a user id or a correction note appears in the output.
- **L-020:** the quality table. For any version, one row per team: games, home and away, games at each start time and on each field, shortest and longest rest, bye weeks. Beside the soft cost it states a floor where one can be counted: on the capstone, eight games in seven weeks force ten short rests, so no schedule scores below 100, and the generator's 114 is 14 above that. The floor counts the rest rule only; the page says so, and says nothing is claimed about the true minimum (D-5, D-34).
- **L-021:** the demo league and the browser test. One command builds Eastside Rec Softball in mid-season: two divisions, 51 games in 56 slots, a published schedule (301 down to 122 against a floor of 100), a score for every game before today and three teams level on points. The season is dated around the day the command runs, because the app only takes scores for past days and only moves future games. The first version gave a different schedule each run with the same seed: team ids were random and the engine sorts by them. Fixed ids made it repeat. One browser test then walks the whole path as the commissioner: the tie resolved in words, a bracket played to a champion, and a rained-out day where the comparison screen lists exactly the displaced games and nothing else. It was the first time the rainout, playoff and quality screens ran in a browser, and none needed a fix.

**L-023, what-if and clinch.** Clinched and eliminated come from trying every outcome of the games left, three per game, and only on points: 531,441 outcomes at the 12-game limit, about 0.4 s for nine teams. Above 12 the page says so and shows nothing. The flags are deliberately conservative: level on points counts against a team for clinching and for it for elimination, so a tiebreaker never makes a claim the enumeration did not check. The cut line is asked on the page because the season does not store how many teams qualify (D-38).

**L-024, calendar feed.** Each team has a calendar address a coach can subscribe to. It is the public page's own data in another format: game times are written as the league's local times with the zone named beside them, so nothing converts a time and nothing can be an hour out. A game keeps the same identity when it is rescheduled, so it moves in the calendar instead of showing twice. It was checked by tests reading the file, not in a calendar app (D-39).

**L-026, crossover games.** A season can add rounds of games between divisions: in each round every team of the smaller division plays one team of the other, never the same opponent twice, and the larger division's teams never differ by more than one game. The scheduler needed one change, and it was in the one scoring function: fairness of start times and fields is counted for each team among its own division, so a crossover game counts for both sides, each in its own table. The owner chose that crossover results count in the standings (D-40). The earlier rule, that a result against an outside team is dropped, stayed as the engine's default with its tests untouched; the season loader opts in. Head-to-head still reads only the games among the tied teams. On the sample league, 2 crossover rounds added 12 games (63 in all) and the run took 547 ms.

**L-025, a model against the generator.** The demo season was given to three Claude models, three times each, with the rules in plain words and a request for a schedule as JSON. The same scoring function that gates publishing scored every answer (D-43).

| | Hard violations | Unplaced | Soft cost (three runs) | Dollars a run | Seconds a run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generator, seed 2027 | 0 | 0 | 122 | 0 | 0.6 |
| Haiku 5.5 | 0 | 0 | 139, 146, 123 | 0.01 to 0.03 | 63 to 234 |
| Sonnet 5.5 | 0 | 0 | 142 to 156 | 0.07 to 0.26 | 37 to 186 |
| Opus 5.5 | 0 | 0 | 102, 100, 100 | 0.61 to 0.86 | 251 to 371 |

What was expected was not what was measured. Every one of the nine answers was a schedule that could be published, and Opus beat the demo's own schedule all three times; two of its answers cost 100, which is the lowest the rest rule allows on this season by counting. The generator's 122 is one seed. Over seeds 1 to 20 it finishes between 100 and 124 and reaches 100 twice, at under a second and no money a seed, so twenty seeds take about twelve seconds against four to six minutes and about 80 cents for one Opus answer. The case for the generator on this evidence is not that a model cannot do it. It is that the generator is free, takes under a second, gives the same schedule for the same seed, and can be run until a counted floor is reached; and that either way the answer has to go through `scoreSchedule` before anyone trusts it. This is one season, one prompt and nine calls ($2.82 in all): it says nothing about a season that cannot fit, pinned games, or a larger league.

**L-027, score entry fixes.** A review of the screens as a scorekeeper found that saving a score form without touching it recorded a 0-0 tie, which then counted in the standings. The form now starts empty and refuses a missing score, names the two teams instead of "home" and "away", opens ready for a correction when a score already exists, marks a tie as a tie, shows entry times in the league's own timezone, and asks for a reason before a playoff score is changed. 405 tests, 2 of them new.

**L-028, results for tonight.** The same review found the score list in no useful order for someone standing at a field with a clipboard. It now opens with the games already played that still have no score, grouped by field, with a count of how many are left; a game that has not been played yet no longer offers a score form.

**L-029, no plain Generate after a score.** A plain run builds the whole season from nothing and knows nothing of games already played; until now it could be run mid-season and only the publish step caught the result. Once any game has a score the buttons are gone and the action refuses, and the page points to "Regenerate the rest", which keeps played games where they were played.
